
A community health clinic in **Córdoba, Argentina** runs a weekly workflow to **schedule specialist consultations** (cardiology, neurology, endocrinology) for patients who come in through a referral.

1. **Intake & triage**

* A patient requests an appointment (phone, WhatsApp, or in person). A receptionist opens a “request” and captures: referral reason, preferred days, insurance status, and urgency.
* A nurse does a quick triage to assign a priority level and confirm the specialty.

2. **Eligibility decision — XOR**

* If the patient **has insurance**, the clinic proceeds with the insurance path.
* If the patient **doesn’t have insurance**, the clinic proceeds with the self-pay path.
* (Only one path applies here, so it’s an **XOR-style decision**.)

3. **Parallel preparation — AND**
   Once the request is accepted, the clinic starts **two things in parallel**:

* The receptionist **reserves a tentative slot** in the calendar with the specialist.
* The nurse/admin **collects clinical prerequisites** (recent lab results, prior studies, medication list) and prepares a short pre-visit summary.
  (This is an **AND-style “do both” step**.)

4. **Optional extra requirements — OR**
   Depending on the case, the clinic may need **one or more** extra actions before confirming:

* If symptoms are severe, the nurse may request an **urgent ECG**.
* If the referral is for a chronic condition, they may request **recent lab work**.
* If the patient is new, they may request **ID verification**.
  Sometimes it’s just one of these; sometimes it’s **two or all three**, so it behaves like an **OR/inclusive decision** (“one or more may be required”).

5. **Waiting on patient/insurer response — Event-based**
   After the clinic sends the patient a message (“Please send documents / confirm you can attend”), the process **waits for whichever event happens first**:

* The patient **replies with the requested documents** (message event), or
* The patient **confirms attendance** (message event), or
* **48 hours pass** with no response (timer event).
  This is an **event-based choice**: the next step depends on which event arrives first.

6. **Confirmation decision — XOR**

* If documents are complete and the patient confirms, the appointment is **confirmed**.
* If documents are incomplete, the clinic **requests missing items** (and returns to the waiting step).
* If the timer expires with no response, the clinic **releases the tentative slot** and marks the request as “not confirmed.”
  (Each outcome is mutually exclusive, so it’s an **XOR-style decision**.)

7. **Day-before actions — AND**
   For confirmed appointments, the day before the visit:

* The receptionist sends **reminders + directions**.
* The nurse prints/exports the **pre-visit summary** and flags any risks.
  (Again, **AND-style parallel work**.)

8. **Visit completion**
   After the consultation, the specialist records outcomes and the clinic closes the request, optionally scheduling follow-up.
